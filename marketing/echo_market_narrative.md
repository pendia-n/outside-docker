# DockDocket Market Orientation Narrative (echo)

DockDocket should be positioned as a **cash-recovery operations tool**, not as inventory software and not as legal-tech. The buyer is not looking for another dashboard. They want fewer denied credits and faster dispute cycles.

## Core Narrative
Small operators already do the hard work by checking deliveries. They still lose because proof is inconsistent. DockDocket does one job: convert receiving events into structured, reusable claim evidence.

## Market Wedge
- Enter through independent stores and small wholesale operators with frequent supplier deliveries.
- Sell outcomes in plain language: “recover more credits” and “close disputes faster.”
- Keep workflow lightweight enough for receiving-time usage.

## Message Hierarchy
1. **Primary promise:** Better evidence, better claim outcomes.
2. **Secondary promise:** Team continuity and accountable records.
3. **Proof point:** Each generated packet is timestamped, structured, and trackable.

## Positioning Boundaries
- Not an ERP replacement.
- Not a contract interpretation engine.
- Not a legal-advice product.
- Not employee surveillance software.

## Pricing Orientation
- Free starter credits to reduce onboarding friction.
- Credit packs align price with realized operational use.
- Later tiers can add multi-location controls and benchmarking.

## Distribution Plan
- Partner-led entry through bookkeeping consultants and distributor-facing advisors.
- Content-led acquisition via “recovered credit” case examples.
- Workflow-led retention by embedding into receiving checklist behavior.

## Expansion Logic
After evidence capture becomes routine, expand into:
- supplier-specific evidence templates,
- root-cause pattern analytics,
- multi-location discrepancy benchmarking.

The growth strategy should stay constraint-driven: preserve speed at intake, preserve trust in billing, preserve defensible data quality.
